# Phase 8 — Scenarios: brief (interview 2026-09-25)

## Decisions (Wan)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Scope | **The 8 Patrol Games** [32.11-32.19] as stand-alone scenarios with the book's victory [32.1], **plus fleet command built and proven on one campaign: The Rising Sun** [31.2] (December 1941 to December 1942, 51 weeks). The other five campaigns follow later on the same machinery. |
| Fleet fights | **You pick, the rest are quick.** Before the week's searches you mark which boats fight on the Combat Display; every other boat on station uses Quick Resolution [27.0], which the book offers for campaign use. There are "fight all" and "quick all" switches. |
| Fleet orders | **Standing orders + exceptions.** You give each boat a patrol area when it sails. After that the teaching captain's standing rules run it (stay on station, search, go home when Endurance says so, nearest base). You're asked only for boats Ready for Sea and for the fights you chose. A weekly Fleet board shows everything. |
| Extras | **Neither** the optional rules [33.0] nor East Wind Early in this phase. |

## The book's terms for the two parts

**Patrol Games [32.0]**: one boat, one patrol from departure to return. No War Events, no War Period or Mk-XIV
change, and "Super Skippers are not used": you are the captain. Following the SOP's note for patrol scenarios
[4.0], the Strategic and Triple-R segments are skipped, so there is no ULTRA either. Victory [32.1]: beat the
ships *or* the tonnage = good captain; both = great; both in fewer weeks = legendary; losing the boat −1 level.
The First Wolfpack [32.19] sails as a three-boat pack (Cero with Shad and Grayback, Momsen's flag in Cero).

**The Rising Sun [31.2]**
- Order of battle [31.13], as Campaign 1:
  - Manila: 26 boats Ready for Sea, with Sealion and Seadragon in Repair Box 1.
  - S-36 in the Lingayen SMZ.
  - Pearl Harbor: Dolphin and Tautog, with Narwhal and Cachalot in Repair Box 3.
  - Five boats in the Hawaiian Islands, on RTB, TF contacts only.
  - Argonaut and Trout at Midway; Tambor and Triton at Wake: two turns there, then RTB.
- Reinforcements [31.01] month by month, through Sunfish in November 1942.
- The first turn skips the Strategic Segment.
- **Career longevity** [31.12]: at the end of February, May, August and November 1942 your tonnage must reach
  90,000 / 220,000 / 360,000 / 620,000 t, or you are relieved of command and the game ends.
- **Victory** [31.24], on tonnage at the end of December 1942:

  | Tonnage | Result |
  |---|---|
  | 800,000+ t | Decisive Victory |
  | 780,000+ t | Substantial Victory |
  | 750,000+ t | Draw |
  | 720,000+ t | Defeat |
  | less | Failure |

  Then modify by Japanese capital ships (CA, BB, CV) sunk: 4+ up two levels, 3 up one, 1-2 none, 0 down one.
  12 or more US boats lost is down one.
- **No off-screen fleet**: every US boat is yours, so the war clock runs on your own sinkings.

## Design calls (Wan may overrule)

- **Special Mission quotas** are filled automatically by the standing orders: as many reachable boats as the event
  asks, nearest first. This follows [5.2] ("as many as possible"). The Fleet board lists who went.
- **Relegation** removes the boats the table names first, then the weakest eligible boats, preferring those in port.
- **Fall of Luzon / Dutch East Indies**: boats in port conduct Base-to-Base movement on the map (they sail on the
  Transit side for Fremantle); Repair Boxes 2-3 and the Hold Box are scuttled; Repair Box 1 limps out damaged [18.3].
- **The Aleutians campaign** designates the eight weakest boats (S-boats first) to Dutch Harbor. Their standing
  orders keep them in the Aleutians and Kuriles.
- **The Hold Box and Max Repair Capacity** [10.1-10.2] and **Congregating Risk** [22.0] now bind for real: every
  boat is on the map.
