## [1.0] INTRODUCTION

Silent War is a **solitaire** simulation of the United States' submarine war against Imperial Japan in WWII.
Scenarios recreate stages of the war or the entire campaign. The player is **Commander Submarines, U.S.
Pacific Fleet (ComSubPac)**, deploying submarines from Pearl Harbor or Australia to attack the Japanese Navy.
The **patrol game** uses single submarines to recreate famous patrols.

### [1.1] How to Read these Rules
The rules follow the sequence of play. Set up, follow the SOP for an overview, then begin play referencing
rules as you go. New players should play a **patrol scenario** before a campaign game. The Comprehensive
Example of Play is at the rear of the scenario book.

*Note: Players must supply four opaque cups.*

### [2.1] The Game Map
Portrays the Pacific Theater with transit routes, operations areas, special mission zones, displays and charts.
Three types of play **Areas**:

- **Submarine Base** — hexagon + port (anchor) symbol.
- **Operations Area (OpArea)** — large circular area containing an Activity Chart. Divided into an
  **Operating** section (top) for subs conducting combat there, and a **Done** section (bottom) for subs that
  have completed combat or movement there.
- **Special Mission Zone (SMZ)** — small circular area with no Activity Chart, pointing to an OpArea. With a
  submarine tender present it is a forward submarine base. With no tender, it can only be entered when
  activated by a War Event.

All Areas are linked by **transit routes** (lines). Only submarines move on the map. Each OpArea belongs to a
geographic group named atop its Activity Chart (e.g. Empire, Indochina, North Pac, South Pac).

### [2.2] Game Charts and Tables
**On the map:** Individual Area Activity Charts, Search and Contact Chart, Transit Event Table, Submarine
Damage Table, Endurance Table.

**On the Charts & Tables card:** Attack Results Table, Counterattack Table, Combat Events Table, Transit Event
Table, Search and Contact Table, Endurance Table, Enemy Submarines Table, Torpedo Improvement Table, War
Period Tables.

**On a separate card:** War Mix Table and ULTRA Table.

**In the rules booklet:** War Event Tables for all 4 War Periods and the East Wind Early scenario.

**Map displays:** Tonnage & Ships Sunk / Submarines Lost Track, Current War Period, Current Torpedo Value
Track, Turn Record Track, and five Base Displays — **Pearl Harbor, Fremantle, Brisbane, Dutch Harbor,
Manila/Subic Bay**. Each Base Display contains a **Repair Hold** box with three levels of patching up, a
**Ready for Sea** box, a **Pier Side** box, and a **Broom Box** (subs just returned from sea).

**Submarine Tender/Surabaya Display card:** eight Submarine Tenders and the Surabaya Display.

### [2.3] Playing Pieces

#### A. SUBMARINES
Every U.S. submarine that served in the Pacific War has its own piece. Two sides: **Transit** (light blue) and
**Patrol** (gray).

**Transit side** — name, class, plus:
- **Entry Date** — when the sub arrives (W/S = "War Start", 12/41).
- **Speed** — number of OpAreas the sub may move in a single turn.
- **Range** — max number of OpAreas the sub may be from any friendly Base at any time.
- **Readiness** — the value the player must roll to "ready" the sub for sea.

**Patrol side** — name, class, plus:
- **Attack** — fighting value (tube count, fore/aft tubes, torpedo loadout).
- **Defense** — maneuver, dive speed, diving depth, tonnage, robustness. Size matters.
- **Tactical** — "handiness" in combat: modernity, engineering responsiveness, combat systems (scopes, TDC,
  radar, sonar), naval-construction experience.
- **Endurance** — determines whether the sub may remain on patrol or must return to base (torpedo loadout,
  food, water, fuel).
- **Range** — max OpAreas from a friendly Base at any time.

#### B. SURFACE SHIPS & EVENT MARKERS
Japanese units are generic pieces reused repeatedly, with type, tonnage, defense, and ASW ratings.

**Ship types:**

| Code | Type |
|---|---|
| CV | Aircraft Carrier |
| BB | Battleship |
| BV | Battleship Carrier (Ise class hybrid) |
| CA | Heavy Cruiser |
| AC | Armored Cruiser |
| AR | Repair Ship |
| CL | Light Cruiser |
| DD | Destroyer |
| DE | Destroyer Escort |
| AO | Oil Tanker |
| AF | Stores ship (often refrigerated) |
| M | Merchant — freighter, transport, and auxiliaries such as minelayers, seaplane tenders, submarine tenders |

- **Tonnage** — size in thousands of Tons Light (ship weight without cargo or fuel).
- **Defense Rating** — how hard the ship is to hit with a torpedo plus its robustness/ability to withstand
  damage; inherent explosion dangers are factored in. **Aircraft have no defense rating — they cannot be attacked.**
- **ASW Rating** — 0, ½, 1, 2, 3, or 4. **The ASW value of a ship with a Damage marker is zero.** Aircraft only
  have an ASW rating.

Ships/aircraft with **white** top and bottom bands are IJN units; **yellow** bands are merchant vessels.

Pieces marked **"COMBAT EVENT"** indicate a unique event has occurred (see [19.2]).

**Named Japanese ships:** carriers *Kaga, Shokaku, Shinano, Taiho*; battleship *Yamato*; repair ship *Asahi*;
armored cruiser *Izumo*. If sunk, these and the BV hybrid battleship/carrier are removed from play.

**Diligent Escorts:** three pieces, printed with an orange DD silhouette on a gray band — particularly alert,
aggressive, tenacious destroyer captains.

#### C. TDC MARKERS
Target Data Computer solutions on a target, with modification values from **−3 to +3**.

#### D. SUPER SKIPPERS
Top U.S. submarine skippers, with a combat bonus rating added into the submarine's attack and defense
calculations. One British and one Dutch skipper are provided for the optional Allied submarine rules.

#### E. MARKERS
Game turn, ships and tonnage sunk, submarines lost, current War Period.

### [2.4] Scale
The map covers the Pacific from Hawaii to the Malay Peninsula, and from Northern Australia to the Aleutians —
somewhat scaleless. **Each game turn is one week.**

### [2.5] Conventions
When rolling the d10, **zero (0) is always zero, not ten**.

Logical terms: `#` = number/value; `>` greater than; `<` less than; `= or >` at least; `= or <` at most;
`drms` = die roll modifications.

**Fraction Rounding:** Silent War uses both rounding-up and rounding-down to stay close to the statistical
model — pay close attention to each rule. When rounding, fractions **< 0.499 round down**, fractions
**≥ 0.5 round up**.

## [3.0] SETTING UP THE GAME

Choose a scenario. Lay out the map and sort submarines by the month printed on each piece from the start
through the end of the scenario (sorting by month/Entry Date is faster than hunting by name). Entry Date
"w/s" = available at war start. Place the beginning month's submarines in their arrival locations, **Transit
side up**. Set Wolfpack and Submarine Tender markers aside (they enter later). Sort the markers, TDC markers,
Super Skippers, and the Japanese ships (naval ensign or national ensign). Japanese ships are distributed among
four cups in a **"War Mix"**.

### [3.1] The "War Mix"
Distribution of Japanese ships into four opaque cups: **A, B, C, D**. Sort Japanese ships by type and tonnage,
then consult the War Mix Table.

**[3.11]** The War Mix Table is divided into the four War Periods, plus an extra column for the bonus 1932
campaign. Each War Period has a column per cup; Japanese ship categories run down the left. Use the columns
for the scenario's War Period. If a ship category (e.g. "M 7k tons or more") contains different piece types,
distribute them **randomly** into the cups. Flag type makes no difference — a CL with a naval ensign and a CL
with a "red ball" flag are distributed randomly. Flags supply fog of war: you can rarely be certain of what you
see through the periscope.

**[3.12]** Nine Japanese pieces have printed **Entry Dates** and are not placed in the cups until then:
BB *Yamato* (5/'42), BV 36t (10/'43), CV *Taiho* (3/'44), CV *Shinano* (11/'44), and the five *Matsu*-class
destroyer escorts (3/'44).

**[3.13]** Italicized Japanese units noted as sunk at scenario start are not placed in the War Mix; they are
removed from the game and not replaced.

**[3.14]** Re-shuffle the War Mix every two months (so the player forgets what is in which cup), and as
directed by the War Mix Table.

### [3.2] Marker Set Up
Place the Tonnage, Ships Sunk, and Submarines Lost markers on the Tonnage & Ships Sunk / Submarines Lost
Track, and the Torpedo, Month, Week, Year, and War Progress markers in their boxes per the scenario.

## [4.0] SEQUENCE OF PLAY

Game Turns represent **one week**. Each turn has Segments; each Segment has Phases. This SOP is for
**operational and campaign scenarios**. For **patrol scenarios, ignore the Strategic and Triple-R segments
except the Prep for Operations Phase [11.0]**.

### I. STRATEGIC SEGMENT
| # | Phase | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 5.0 | War Event | Roll d10; if 0–3, reroll on the War Events Table and implement results. |
| 6.0 | Torpedo Improvement | Roll d10 on the Torpedo Improvement Table. If result = or > the Ships Sunk #, move the torpedo marker one column right. |
| 7.0 | ULTRA | Roll d10 on the ULTRA Table, noting drms. Mark ULTRA Areas: +1 to Area Activity Chart rolls and draw one extra ship from each cup. |

### II. TRIPLE 'R' SEGMENT (Reinforcement, Ready, Repair)
| # | Phase | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 8.0 | Submarine Reinforcement | Consult the Master Submarine Reinforcement List. Place and remove submarines. |
| 9.0 | Ready Submarines (bases and sub tenders) | Move subs in the Pierside Box to Ready for Sea. Roll for subs in the Broom Box: roll = or < Ready # → Ready for Sea Box; roll > Ready # → Pierside Box. |
| 10.0 | Submarine Repair (bases) | Roll = or < the # in the Repair Box to move the sub to the next box (3→2→1→Broom). |
| 11.0 | Prep For Operations | Move all submarines from the "Done" section of each OpArea to the "Operating" section. |

### III. OPERATIONS SEGMENT *(conduct individually for each sub)*
| # | Phase | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 12.0 | Patrol Movement | Deployed subs Patrol side up may move to the "Operating" section of an adjacent OpArea, or an SMZ if currently active. Roll for a Transit Event. |
| 13.0 | Search and Contact | Follow the on-map Search and Contact instructions (+1 drm for ULTRA, +2 drm for a barrier wolfpack, etc.). |
| 14.0 | Combat | 14.1 First Attack Round · 14.2 Counterattack Round · 14.3 Withdrawal (→14.4 or 14.5) · 14.4 Re-Attack Rounds (optional, followed by Withdrawal) · 14.5 Super Skipper Determination · 14.6 Sub vs. Sub Combat · 14.7 Check Endurance |
| 15.0 | Transit Movement | Subs in a base's Ready for Sea Box, outbound to a patrol area, or in an OpArea's Operating section with an RTB marker may move up to their movement rating in OpAreas. Roll for a Transit Event if applicable. Place the sub in the "Done" section Patrol side up (patrolling there next turn) or Transit side up (continuing on). |

### IV. END TURN SEGMENT *(after all subs have moved and fought)*
| # | Phase | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 16.0 | Conclude Turn | Remove Spotted markers on a roll of 0–6; automatically remove them on return to Base. Check scenario victory conditions. Advance date markers. |
| 17.0 | War Progress | Check the War Period Transition Table. If the month has a number, roll d10; if result = or > that #, advance the War Period. |

## [5.0] WAR EVENT PHASE

At the start of each turn roll one die. On **0, 1, 2, or 3**, roll again on the War Events Table and follow the
event's instructions.

**[5.1]** Some events are **one-time only** (e.g. the fall of Manila). Once they have occurred, further rolls of
that event are treated as No Event.

**[5.2]** Some War Events direct the player to deploy a number of submarines to an SMZ for the turn. These subs
use Patrol Movement to enter the SMZ. If not enough subs can enter via Patrol Movement [12.0], non-RTB subs
that are **Transit side up in or adjacent to** an OpArea with the SMZ must be flipped to Patrol side and moved
into the SMZ. *(This is the ONLY time a Transit side sub may be flipped to Patrol side at the beginning of the
turn, before combat.)* If the player still cannot fulfil the event, there is **no penalty**, but as many
submarines as possible must be moved, up to the number indicated.

## [6.0] TORPEDO IMPROVEMENT PHASE

> **Commentary.** The Mark XIV torpedo came from the Newport Torpedo Station, which obstinately refused to
> acknowledge experienced submariners' complaints, instead accusing skippers of "improperly" setting the
> weapon. Careful experiments in July 1942 in Australia and the intercession of Admiral King finally moved
> Newport to act — but the Mk-XIV did not work as intended until fully a year later, 21 months into the war.

The Mk-XIV marker (also representing the Mk-X used by S-boats and the late-war Mk-XVIII electric) begins on the
Current Torpedo Value track per the scenario. The Torpedo Improvement Table lists ships sunk in tens and a
series of die rolls.

In the Torpedo Improvement Phase of the turn **immediately following** a turn when current ships sunk reaches
or exceeds one of the listed numbers, roll a d10. If the roll **equals or exceeds** the listed result, the
torpedo marker advances immediately. If the roll is less, there is no change and **another roll may not be
attempted until the next listed number of ships sunk is reached**.

> *Example:* In 1942 the player sinks seventy ships, becoming eligible to attempt improvement from −2 to −1.
> The roll is 6 — no improvement (a 9 was required). The player may not roll again until seventy-five ships
> have been sunk, where an 8 or 9 improves the torpedo to −1.

## [7.0] THE ULTRA PHASE

> **Commentary.** Ultra broke the German codes; Magic read the Japanese Navy codes; their combined output came
> to be known as "ULTRAs". Throughout the war the Ultras gave the submarine arm dramatically accurate
> information on Japanese convoy movements. Early lack of success was not from being in the wrong place, but
> from defective torpedoes.

**Procedure.** At the start of each turn, consult the ULTRA Table: roll a die and find the result under the
current War Period. Place an ULTRA marker in each listed Area.

- All die rolls on the Area Activity Charts in those Areas are **increased by one (+1)**.
- All numbers from the Engagement Table (the numbers placed in columns A, B, C, D) are **increased by one**,
  except cups indicating "no draw".

**[7.1]** When the **Sea of Japan** OpArea contains an ULTRA marker, Transit Event Check die rolls use a **+1**
modifier instead of the normal +2.

**[7.2]** ULTRA Areas last **one turn only**; new ULTRA Areas are rolled at the beginning of each turn.

**[7.3]** There may never be more than **three (3)** ULTRA Areas in one turn. If two rolls are made due to a
War Event, take the Areas in the order received, top to bottom.

> *Example:* In War Period 3 a War Event calls for rolling twice on the ULTRA table. The player rolls 2 then 4.
> ULTRA markers go on Empire Pacific, Bismarck Sea, and the Kuriles. No marker is placed in the Carolines.

**[7.4]** Submarines are **never required** to respond to ULTRA information. Choosing to Patrol Move into a
target-rich area is the player's decision, balanced against the risks of Patrol Movement [12.0].

## [8.0] SUBMARINE REINFORCEMENTS PHASE

> **Commentary.** The U.S. began a massive submarine building program in coastal and Great Lakes yards. Nearly
> every submarine through the Gato class received a major overhaul during the war — often repairing battle
> damage beyond the capability of Pacific bases. Overhauls do not alter ratings but are a major logistics
> impact, so they are part of the game.

Consult the **Master Submarine Reinforcements list**. Four categories:

- **A. Additions to War Mix** — Japanese units with entry dates are added to the War Mix per the War Mix Table.
- **B. Reinforcements** — newly received submarines, listed with their base of arrival. Place them in the
  **Broom Box** of that Base display. If the base is not yet active or has fallen, they are received at Pearl
  Harbor (*exception:* Brisbane subs are received at Fremantle if Fremantle is active).
- **C. Withdrawals** — subs withdrawn for overhaul on the U.S. West Coast, preceded by "Withdraw". Removed from
  play **immediately**, even if at sea and/or damaged; place in the **"West Coast" Box** on the map. Any Super
  Skipper stays with the boat; damaged/spotted markers are removed. If the sub has been sunk, no other sub
  takes its place. While in overhaul a sub cannot be removed by the War Event "Relegation to Training Duty".
- **D. Returns** — after some months, overhauled subs return, preceded by "Return". All returning submarines
  are placed in the **Broom Box of Pearl Harbor** unless otherwise noted.

## [9.0] READYING SUBMARINES FOR SEA PHASE

> **Commentary.** Readying represents provisioning, fueling, and assembling/training the crew — indeterminate
> in length. The average Pacific submarine turned around between patrols in a week, sometimes two. Older,
> "creakier" boats took more effort than new war-construction sisters.

**Procedure.** Move all submarines in the **Pier Side Box** of a Base (**Shipside Box** of a Tender) to the
**Ready for Sea Box**. For each submarine starting in the **Broom Box**, roll a die:

- Roll **= or < Readiness** → move to the **Ready for Sea Box**.
- Roll **> Readiness** → move to the **Pier Side Box**, where it will be automatically ready for sea next turn.

## [10.0] REPAIR PHASE

Submarines with a Damage marker may be repaired at a Base. Each Base's Repair Box has four sub-boxes:
**Hold**, **3**, **2**, **1**. A Repair Number is printed in boxes 3, 2, and 1.

**Repair Procedure.** Roll a die for each submarine in a Repair Box, **starting with Repair Box 1 and advancing
one box at a time toward the Hold Box**. If the roll is **= or <** that box's Repair number, advance the sub to
the next box. A sub in box "1" advances into the **Broom Box**.

**[10.1] Base Max Repair Capacity** is the total number of submarines that may be in boxes 1, 2, and 3 at any
one time. A damaged sub may not be advanced into the repair process unless there is room for it.

**[10.2]** If a sub takes damage of 1 or more and the Base is already at Max Repair Capacity, excess subs must
go in the **Hold Box**. Which subs go there is up to the player, but it usually makes sense to repair a sub with
less damage first, shunting a more damaged sub into the Hold Box even if it arrived earlier.

**[10.21]** When repairs free space, subs may be advanced from the Hold Box into box **"3"** — regardless of the
actual damage suffered, it is assumed to have deteriorated while waiting in the holding basin — but must wait
until the following turn to roll for repair.

> *Note:* Plan for the return of damaged subs to avoid the holding-basin penalty. Subs operating out of Pearl
> Harbor can "bounce" to Australia if needed.

**[10.3]** Submarines waiting in a Repair Box **may not leave a Base**. If a Base falls with a submarine in
Repair Box 2 or 3 or the Hold Box, see [18.3].

## [12.0] PATROL MOVEMENT PHASE

On patrol, submarines normally stay in one OpArea, but the player may change patrol area to an **adjacent**
OpArea via Patrol Movement. Move the sub to any adjacent OpArea, so long as it does not place the sub **further
from a Base than its Range rating**. Then roll for a Transit Event [19.1]. On an **RTB** result, flip the sub to
its Transit side and place an RTB marker on it.

**[12.1]** If an SMZ has been activated by a War Event, a submarine may enter it using Patrol Movement for **no
additional movement cost**. Roll for a Transit Event.

> *Example:* A Special Mission Event has occurred for a Guadalcanal Patrol. A sub on patrol in the Coral Sea may
> Patrol Move to the Solomons, then enter the Guadalcanal SMZ at no additional cost, rolling for a Transit Event
> using the **Solomons** Area Activity Chart.

**[12.2]** Submarines may **not** use Patrol Movement to enter or depart a submarine base.

## [13.0] SEARCH AND CONTACT PHASE

> **Commentary.** Submarine combat is essentially sorting through available contacts and quickly determining
> the best opportunities. Information is usually imperfect, time short, angles and opportunities limited.

### [13.1] Activity Determination Step
A submarine may choose to **bypass** the Search and Contact Procedure to avoid combat (spending its time
submerged and/or avoiding surface traffic). If so: remove any Spotted marker from the sub and proceed directly
to the Endurance Phase [14.7] with a **−1 modifier** to the Endurance roll.

The player may also **voluntarily RTB** in this step: place an RTB marker on the sub, skip directly to the
Transit Movement Phase [15.0], and move the sub toward the desired base.

> *Note:* This is the only time a submarine can enter RTB status **and** conduct RTB transit movement in the
> same turn. If a sub enters RTB status due to combat, event, or endurance, it is placed in the **DONE** section
> of the OpArea.

### [13.2] Search and Contact Procedure

**A. Area Activity Chart.** Consult the Area Activity Chart of the OpArea containing the submarine. If the sub
is in an SMZ activated by a War Event, use the AAC of the OpArea **from which it entered** the SMZ. If there are
any colored boxes in the row for the current war period, roll a die adding these **cumulative** modifiers:

- **+1** if this is an ULTRA area [7.0]
- **+2** if rolling for a **Barrier** deployed wolfpack
- **−1** if the submarine is **Spotted** and it is War Period 1 or 2

Box color = Activity Level:

| Color | Activity Level |
|---|---|
| White | No contact |
| Green | Contact with a **very small** number of enemy ships |
| Orange | Contact with a **small** number of enemy ships |
| Blue | Contact with a **moderate** number of enemy ships |
| Red | Contact with a **large** number of enemy ships |

Two exceptions before proceeding to Step B:

- If the die roll is a **zero**, the box is **white**, and the row has at least one colored box → roll a second
  die. If that is **also zero**, the submarine has encountered an **enemy submarine** → go to Sub vs. Sub Combat
  [14.6].
- If there is **no contact** → proceed directly to the Endurance Phase [14.7] with a **−1** modifier to the
  Endurance roll.

**B. Contact Table.** Once enemy ships are contacted, roll on the Contact Table (left side of map), first
determining the War Period, then rolling a die. Result is **C1** (small convoy), **C2** (large convoy), or
**TF** (naval task force).

**C. Engagement Table.** Cross-index the Activity Level (from the AAC roll) with the Contact result. The
Engagement Table directs the player to randomly select a number of pieces from each of the four War Mix cups
A, B, C, D. Draw pieces **without examining them** and place them **face down** on the Combat Display in the
column corresponding to their cup.

If the OpArea has an **ULTRA** marker, the number of pieces per cup is **increased by one**, unless that cup's
result was "no draw".

## [14.0] COMBAT PHASE

The player engages the Japanese convoy or task force in a series of attack rounds.

### [14.1] First Attack Round

**[14.11]** Place the submarine in one of the **submarine boxes** on the Combat Display. **No more than one
submarine per box.** See also [24.5] Wolfpacks and Combat.

**[14.12] Reveal.** Reveal a number of Japanese units equal to the submarine's **Tactical Rating**, turning them
face up one at a time. Revealed ships must be from **the submarine's own column or an adjacent one**.

- If a **Combat Event** marker is revealed, resolve it immediately per [19.2]. Any additional Combat Event
  markers drawn during an attack round are returned to the cup.
- If a **Diligent Escort** is revealed, roll immediately on the **Diligent Escort** row of the Counterattack
  Table and apply the result. Combat then proceeds normally with the Diligent Escort face up in its proper
  column. **If the submarine is damaged, the player may choose to withdraw** at this point, proceeding to the
  Endurance Phase [14.7].

**Radar (1943–45).** The submarine may **"unflip"** any one Japanese unit — unless it is a Combat Event,
Diligent Escort, or Aircraft. The choice must be made **immediately, before any other units are revealed**. If a
unit is unflipped, another unit must be revealed in its place.

**[14.13] TDC placement.** The submarine's **Tactical Rating** also determines the number of TDC markers
randomly selected from the face-down pool. Place TDC markers **without examining them**, one per target, on any
**revealed or face-down** Japanese ships in the submarine's column or an adjacent column. **Once placed, TDC
markers may not be moved.**

**[14.14] Target selection.** Reveal the TDC markers and select targets. A target must be a unit **with a TDC
marker on it**. The player may select as many targets as he can allocate the submarine's attack value to, with a
minimum of one. **Total attacks may not exceed the submarine's attack value.** A submarine may only attack
targets in **the same or an adjacent column**.

> *Example:* USS *Spearfish* (6-3-4) has Whitaker (+1) as super skipper, in combat against two 2-0 cargo ships,
> one 1-0 cargo ship, and one revealed 7-1 destroyer. One 2-0 cargo ship has TDC −2 but is in an adjacent
> column; the other has TDC +1. The 1-0 cargo ship has TDC 0. The player assigns 3 attack points to the first
> cargo maru and 3 to the third maru, and does not target the second target or the escort. Total attack points
> assigned: 6.

Once targets are selected, any **unrevealed** targeted Japanese unit is turned face up. If a Japanese **aircraft
or Combat Event** marker is revealed this way, remove the TDC marker from it; attack points allocated to it may
be reallocated to any other unit with a TDC marker.

- If a **Combat Event** is revealed, resolve immediately per [19.2]; additional Combat Event markers drawn in
  the round are returned to the cup.
- If a **Diligent Escort** is revealed, roll immediately on the Diligent Escort row of the Counterattack Table
  and apply the result. Combat proceeds normally with it face up in its column. **If the submarine is damaged,
  the player may NOT withdraw at this point.**

**[14.15] Attack resolution** — resolve each attack separately, steps A–D:

**A. Attacker total:**
1. The portion of the submarine's Attack Value allocated to this target.
2. The value of a Super Skipper marker assigned to this submarine (if any). **Each attack gets this bonus.**
3. The current Mk-XIV torpedo value (adding a negative number = subtracting it).

**B. Target total:**
1. The target's Defense Value.
2. The **ASW value of all revealed Japanese vessels** in the submarine's column and adjacent columns, plus the
   ASW value of **all revealed aircraft regardless of column**.
3. The TDC marker value.
4. **+1** if the target ship is not in the same column as the submarine. *(Note: maximum two submarines per
   column.)*
5. **−1** if the target ship has a Damage marker.

**C. Resolve:** Subtract the target's value from the attacker's value (**round down**) to get a result, and roll
a die. If the roll is **= or <** the result, the target is **hit**.

> *Example (continuing, Mk-XIV value −1):*
> **Cargo Ship #1 (2-0, adjacent column):** a) 3 attack + 1 super skipper + (−1) torpedo = **+3**.
> b) 2 defense + 1 revealed escort + (−2) TDC + 1 adjacent column = **+2**.
> c) Must roll = or < (3−2) = **1** to hit (20% chance).
> **Cargo Ship #3 (1-0, same column):** a) 3 + 1 + (−1) = **+3**. b) 1 defense + 1 revealed escort + 1 TDC =
> **+3**. c) Must roll = or < (3−3) = **0** to hit (10% chance).

**D. Attack Results Table.** If hit, consult the ART: find the target's tonnage size in the left column and roll
a die, **adding the current Mk-XIV torpedo value**.

- **Orange result** → target **damaged**; place a Damage marker. If it already had a Damage marker, it is **sunk**.
- **Red result** → target **sunk**. Advance the Ships Sunk marker by one and the Tonnage Sunk marker(s) by the
  tonnage size.

> *Note:* The torpedo value counts twice — first for hitting (running at the proper depth), then for detonating
> correctly (the problematic magnetic exploder / defective contact exploders).

**[14.16] Impossible attacks.** Sometimes a sub cannot possibly hit a target (low attack, low torpedo rating,
adverse TDC, high escorts):

- **A.** If **no successful attack can be made in the first attack round**, there is **no Counterattack** [14.2].
  The player may withdraw [14.3] or conduct a Re-Attack round [14.4] normally.
- **B.** If no successful attack can be made in a **Re-Attack** round, a counterattack round is conducted
  normally regardless of whether an attack was made in the first attack round.

> *Note:* Unrevealed units with TDC markers are considered to have **defense 1** until revealed. If attacked and
> it is then determined the target cannot be hit, the attack goes through as an **automatic miss**, followed by a
> counterattack.

### [14.2] Counterattack Round

Counterattack normally occurs after the submarine has attacked surface ships, but in certain circumstances may
occur before and after. *Units sunk in the preceding attack round are removed prior to Counterattack.*

**A. Japanese total:**
1. The **ASW value of all revealed Japanese forces** (even those not attacked) in the submarine's column and all
   adjacent columns, **fractions rounded up**. The ASW value of a ship with a Damage marker is **zero**. Aircraft
   ASW is added **regardless of column**.
2. The **number of red boxes** for the current war period in the AAC of the OpArea where combat occurs.
3. The **general ASW value** from the War Period display for the current war period.

**B. Submarine total:**
1. The submarine's Defense value.
2. The value of a Super Skipper marker assigned to this submarine (if any).

**C. Resolve:** Subtract the submarine's result from the Japanese result and find that row on the Counterattack
Table. Roll a die with these modifiers:
1. **+1** for each Damage marker the submarine has.
2. **+1** if this Counterattack follows a Re-Attack.
3. **+1** if the submarine has a Spotted marker.

Some table results require a second roll on the Counterattack Table. Apply results immediately.

> *Example (Spearfish, War Period 2, East China Sea, general ASW 1):*
> a) 1 revealed ASW + 3 red boxes in the AAC + 1 general ASW = **+5**.
> b) 3 submarine defense + 1 Super Skipper = **+4**.
> c) Consult the **'1'** line of the Counterattack Table (5 − 4 = 1).

### [14.3] Withdrawal
Once the Counterattack Round is over, the player may voluntarily withdraw the submarine from combat. If so,
proceed to Super Skipper Determination [14.5].

### [14.4] Re-Attack Rounds

If the submarine has not been sunk, withdrawn, or forced to RTB, it may engage the convoy/task force a second
time. TDC markers from the first round are removed and mixed back into the draw pool. **Revealed Japanese units
remain revealed.**

**Procedure.** Prior to Re-Attack combat the player may **re-position the submarine to an adjacent column**.
Then repeat [14.12]–[14.16] followed by [14.2], with these exceptions:

- **A. Reveal order:** Ships with the **Rising Sun battle flag** must be revealed first (if in eligible columns).
  Only when all Rising Sun flags have been revealed may a **"red ball"** flag be turned over.
- **B. Radar (1943–45):** the submarine may **not** "unflip" a Japanese unit that was revealed in the first
  attack round.

In the Re-Attack round, **one is subtracted from every TDC marker except any −3 markers** (the submarine
maneuvering for a better shot).

**[14.41]** The first Re-Attack is at the player's option. A **second** Re-Attack may only occur if the sub has a
Super Skipper and the player rolls **= or <** the Super Skipper's rating.

**[14.42]** Once a second Re-Attack concludes, combat ends [14.3].

### [14.5] Super Skipper Determination

If the submarine sank **three (3) or more ships totaling 16,000 or more tons** during the Combat Phase, it has a
Super Skipper.

- No Super Skipper yet → draw one randomly and assign it at the **+1** side.
- Already **+1** → flip to the **+2** side.
- Already **+2** → no additional benefit.

See [25.0] for additional rules.

### [14.6] Sub vs. Sub Combat

If Search and Contact produced an enemy submarine encounter:

- **A.** If a wolfpack is involved, randomly select **one** player submarine to make the encounter. The others
  are not involved (this does not affect wolfpack membership).
- **B.** Roll a die: **−1** if the player submarine is SPOTTED and/or DAMAGED; **+1** if it has radar (1943–45).
  Result **0–3** → the enemy spotted the player submarine first. **4–9** → the player detected the enemy first.
- **C.** Consult the **Enemy Submarine Table [30.3]** in the scenario booklet for available enemy submarine
  pieces by date and OpArea; draw one randomly from a cup.
- **D.** Place the submarine that detected the other in the **Submarine Box** of any column of the Combat
  Display, and the detected submarine in a **Target Box** of the same column. Draw and reveal a TDC marker for
  the target submarine.
- **E.** Conduct **one attack round**: the firing sub uses its **full attack rating** plus any Super Skipper
  value against the target submarine's **Defense** rating plus any Super Skipper value. Allied submarines use
  the current Mk-XIV torpedo value unless assigned another value by special rule (early Dutch). **Japanese,
  German, and Italian torpedoes have a value of +1 regardless of date.**
- **F.** If the target submarine is hit, consult the **1-4 row** of the Attack Results Table. If the player
  submarine is sunk, advance the Subs Lost marker by one. If an Axis submarine is sunk, advance the Ships Sunk
  marker by one and the Tonnage Sunk marker by the appropriate amount.

Sub vs. Sub Combat ends after a **single** combat round — no Counterattack and no Re-Attack.

### [14.7] Endurance Check

> **Commentary.** Four elements keep a WWII warship at sea: fuel, victuals, crew, and weapons loadout. Given the
> small number of torpedoes carried (12–24) and the Mk-XIV's problems, the most common limitation was torpedo
> loadout.

**Procedure.** After a turn's combat(s), cross-index the submarine's **Endurance** with the current **Mk-XIV
torpedo value** on the Endurance Table and roll a die.

- **−1** if the submarine bypassed the Search and Contact Phase [13.1], or encountered no enemy contacts.
- **+1** if the submarine is damaged.

Then:
- Result **higher** than the number indicated → mark the sub **RTB** and place it in the **DONE** section of the
  OpArea.
- Result **= or <** the number → place the sub in the **"Done"** section of the OpArea.

### [14.8] Clean-Up

Return Japanese units and Combat Event markers to the appropriate cups. If any of the **italicized** War Mix
ships (*Shokaku, Taiho, Shinano, Yamato, Kaga, Asahi, Izumo*, or the BV) were sunk, they are **removed from the
game** and not placed back into the cups. Remove TDCs from the Combat Display and return them to the draw pool.

## [15.0] TRANSIT MOVEMENT PHASE

Transit Movement is the **only** way a submarine may move to and from a Base. Submarines move along the light
blue lines connecting Areas, and are placed on their **Transit side**. Two types: **Deployment** and **Return to
Base (RTB)**.

### [15.1] Deployment — Base to OpArea, or Base to Base
A submarine starting the Operations Segment in a Base's **Ready for Sea Box** may move any number of OpAreas up
to its **speed**.

> *Note:* Departure from a **permanent base** to an adjacent OpArea **counts** against speed; departure from an
> **SMZ base** to a connected OpArea does **not**, since the SMZ is considered to be "in" the OpArea.

Perform a Transit Event roll [19.1] using the OpArea with the **lowest number on the line for the current War
Period which contains a red box** through which the submarine moved. **Do not use the OpArea from which the
submarine started.** If the sub survives undamaged, place it in the **"Done"** section of the ending OpArea —
Transit side up to transit further next turn, or Patrol side up to patrol next turn. **Once in patrol status a
submarine may never enter transit status again unless returning to base.**

**[15.11]** In following turns a Transit-side-up sub repeats transit movement (and the Transit Event roll). A
submarine may **never move farther from an active base than its range value**.

### [15.2] Return to Base (RTB) — OpArea to Base
Voluntary, or as a Combat/Transit Event result. Such a submarine is marked **RTB** and **can never return to
patrol status without readying at a Base**.

**[15.21]** Submarines may choose to RTB by skipping the Search and Contact and Combat Phases, moving via
OpAreas to the **Broom Box** of the Base (see also [18.0] and [21.0]). Perform a Transit Event roll using the
OpArea with the lowest number on the AAC with red boxes.

- Moving from an OpArea to an **adjacent Base** (e.g. Hawaiian Islands → Pearl Harbor): **no Transit Event roll**.
- If the sub cannot reach base in one move, leave it in the **"Done"** section of the closest reachable OpArea;
  in following Transit Movement Phases it repeats Transit Movement until it reaches the Broom Box of the
  destination Base, conducting applicable Transit Events.

**[15.22] Forced RTB** (Transit Event, Combat, or Endurance Table): flip to Transit side and place an RTB
marker. Place in the **DONE** section of the current OpArea; **in the following turn** it must conduct transit
movement to any base within range. If undamaged → **Broom Box**. If damaged → determine damage level before
being placed in a Repair Box (see [18.0]).

**[15.23] Dutch Harbor.** Operations out of Dutch Harbor were extremely hard on submarines and crews — frigid
temperatures meant extended repairs and increased wear; turnarounds sometimes exceeded a month. **Any
undamaged submarine that RTBs to Dutch Harbor must roll on the Damage Table.** Any result of Repair 2 or
Repair 3 is changed to **Repair 1**.

**[15.24] Cautious Transit.** A Transit-side-up submarine may choose to move a **single** OpArea instead of at
higher speed. If it does, **subtract one** from the Transit Event check [19.1].

**[15.25] Submarines Beyond the Range of a Base.** Submarines may not move beyond their range to a base, but
events (such as the fall of a base) may leave one out of range. Such a submarine is **immediately damaged** and
must RTB to the nearest base using normal transit movement at a speed **one (1) less than printed (but never
less than 1)** — the crew using long-range cruise methods such as single-engine slow running.

- **[16.1]** Check victory conditions. If victory or defeat is achieved, play ends immediately.
- **[16.2] Spotted Marker Removal.** Roll a die for every submarine at sea with a Spotted marker, subtracting
  the rating of its Super Skipper if any. On **0–6** remove the Spotted marker; on **7–9** it remains spotted.
  Only **one roll** is made for a spotted wolfpack — the entire pack either stays spotted or is cleared.
- **[16.3]** Upon entry to a Base, any Spotted marker is removed.
- **[16.4]** Remove ULTRA marker(s) from the map.
- **[16.5]** Advance the Week marker, and the Month/Year markers if needed.

## [17.0] WAR PROGRESS PHASE

> **Commentary.** Silent War assumes the inevitable defeat of Japan as a key underlying component of the system.
> The war is portrayed in four periods: **W1** Pearl Harbor→Midway, **W2** Midway→end of the Solomons campaign,
> **W3** Solomons→Leyte Gulf, **W4** Leyte Gulf→Tokyo Bay. These do not follow historical dates precisely, but
> roughly parallel changes in Japanese naval and shipping movement, on which the AACs and Search & Contact are
> based.

### [17.1] Procedure
The War Period marker starts on the War Period Track per the scenario. At the beginning of months marked with
numbers on the **War Period Transition Table**, the player may check for advancement:

- **A.** Consult the **War Period Modification Table**. Ships & Tonnage Sunk totals can advance or retard the
  war's progress. Under the section for the **next** war period, cross-index Ships Sunk and Tonnage Sunk to get
  the modifier, **rounding DOWN** unless already at the lowest level.
- **B.** Consult the **War Period Transition Table** and roll a die, adding the Step A modifier. If the result is
  **= or >** the number in the box, the War Period advances.
- **C.** If it fails to advance, another attempt may not be made until the beginning of the following month.

### [17.2] Automatic Advance
If the War Period has not advanced by the time all transition numbers have been attempted (e.g. November 1942),
it advances **automatically**.

## [18.0] DAMAGED SUBMARINES

Submarines may be damaged during Transit Events or combat. Place a **Damage marker**; the **actual level of
damage is determined when the submarine reaches a base**.

**[18.1] Second Damage marker** — roll a die:
- Roll **>** the submarine's Defense rating → **sunk**; advance the Submarines Sunk marker by one.
- Otherwise place a second Damage marker. On reaching base, determine damage level (see [10.0]) by rolling
  **twice** on the Damage Table and **adding** the resulting Repair Box numbers. If total damage would place it
  beyond Repair Box "3", the submarine is a **constructive total loss** — removed from play, but it does
  **not** count against submarines sunk.

**[18.2] Third Damage marker** → the submarine is **sunk**; advance the Submarines Sunk marker by one.

**[18.3] Arrival at base.** Damaged submarines reaching base roll one die on the **Damage Table**, placing the
sub in a Repair Box or the Broom Box. **Damaged submarines may not depart a major base once they have arrived.**
For **Manila and Surabaya**, damaged subs in Repair Boxes 2 or 3 or the Hold Box when the base falls are
**scuttled** and treated as constructive total losses — **do not** advance the Lost Submarine marker.
Submarines in Repair Box 1 may limp out to sea: they receive a Damage marker and must RTB to the nearest base,
rolling again on the Damage Table on arrival, though a result of "Superficial Damage" is treated as "Light
Damage", placing them in Repair Box 1.

**[18.4] Decommissioning.** The player may decommission a badly damaged submarine at any time while on the
repair track. It is removed from the game but **not** counted as lost. If it has a Super Skipper, determine
reassignment per [25.3].

**[18.5] Damaged Surface Ships.** The ASW value of a ship with a Damage marker is **zero**, and a **−1** modifier
applies to the target value during the Combat Phase. If a damaged surface ship survives all combat, the damage
has no further effect — remove the Damage marker; the ship is assumed to have made port.

## [19.0] EVENTS

> **Commentary.** Combat is governed by that intangible element called fate, and submarine warfare is
> particularly susceptible to it. Asked whether he would choose a good general or a lucky general, Napoleon
> replied: *"The lucky one, every time!"*

Two types: **Transit Events** and **Combat Events**. Resolve immediately when summoned.

### [19.1] Transit Events

Transit Events reflect submarines spotted and attacked before they knew of the attacker, or losses to "causes
unknown" — torpedoed by a Japanese submarine, attacked by aircraft, mined, or accidental loss such as a battery
explosion or catastrophic mechanical failure.

**A Transit Event check occurs during Transit and Patrol Movement any time a submarine enters an OpArea or SMZ.**

**Governing OpArea:**
- **Patrol Movement** → the OpArea the submarine moved into.
- **SMZ** → the OpArea from which the submarine entered the SMZ.
- **Transit Movement** → the OpArea with the **lowest number on the line for the current War Period which
  contains a red box** through which the sub moved. **Do not use the OpArea it started from.**

Roll a die with these **cumulative** modifiers:

| Modifier | Condition |
|---|---|
| **−1** | Submarine is Transit side up (not performing Patrol Movement) |
| **−1** | Conducting Cautious Transit [15.24] |
| **−1** | Member of a wolfpack [24.4] |
| **+1** | Submarine is Spotted, during War Periods 1 and 2 [23.0] |
| **+2** | Entering a Special Mission Zone [20.3], or entering/leaving the Sea of Japan [7.1] |
| **+1 or more** | Congregating Risk [22.1] |

If the roll **= or >** the number at which **red results start**, a Transit Event has occurred. Roll again
**twice** on the Transit Event Table (on the map) to find the row and column, cross-reference, and apply the
result immediately.

**[19.11]** On an **RTB** result the submarine is placed in the **DONE** section of the OpArea used to determine
the Transit Event check, even if already conducting RTB movement. If more than one OpArea could have been used
(same lowest number with a red box), the player chooses.

### [19.2] Combat Events

Each cup contains one **"Combat Event"** chit. When revealed, play stops and the Event resolves immediately.
**The results of the Event supersede the rules in all cases.** An Event applies to a single attack round, and
**only one Event can occur per attack round**.

Roll a die and consult the table:

| d10 | Event |
|---|---|
| **0** | **Target Lumbers into Way** — a Japanese ship crosses the path of one or more torpedoes. Conduct the Combat Phase normally, but **before** the first attack round [14.15], choose any face-down **"red ball"** flagged ship in this or an adjacent column, reveal it, and determine if it is sunk: roll a die and add the current torpedo modifier on the correct target-size line of the ART. Continue the attack round normally. If no eligible ships are on the display, ignore this Event. |
| **1** | **Surface Gunnery Combat** — the sub surfaces for gun combat. Conduct the Combat Phase normally, but instead of the attack round: 1) place a **Spotted** marker on the submarine; 2) choose any **revealed Japanese merchant/transport** piece in this column and attack it with a die roll on the **26-35** line of the ART (*Argonaut, Nautilus, Narwhal* use the **18-25** line; *Surcouf* the **10-17** line; *Leviathan* the **5-9** line). **The Mk-XIV value is NOT applied.** If there is no revealed merchant/transport piece, or if a revealed IJN unit is present (white aircraft, escort, CL, BB, etc., or a Diligent Escort), this event is ignored. Then proceed to the Counterattack Round. |
| **2** | **Circular Running Torpedo** — conduct the Attack Round normally, but also conduct an attack on **your own submarine**: add one to the current Mk-XIV torpedo value plus a specially drawn TDC marker, and roll a die. If the roll is **= or <** the result, the submarine is **sunk**. If it survives, proceed to the Counterattack Round. |
| **3** | **Betty Bomber Spots Submarine** — roll immediately on the **"Event Aircraft"** line of the Counterattack Table and apply results. Proceed to the Endurance check. |
| **4** | **Mechanical Troubles End Attack** — no Combat Phase for the submarine this turn. Roll a die: **odd** → place a Damage marker **and** an RTB marker; **even** → the chief engineer fixes it and the sub resumes normal operations next turn (it does **not** roll on the Endurance Table this turn). |
| **5** | **Perfect Setup** — proceed normally except **all TDC markers this round have negative values** (treat all "+" as "−"). |
| **6** | **Tonnage Doubled** — at the end of the Combat Phase, **double the tonnage of the largest merchant ship sunk**. |
| **7** | **Counterattack - Combat** — the escorts attack first; then, if not sunk or forced to RTB, the submarine attacks. Conduct [14.11]–[14.14], then skip to the Counterattack Round [14.2]. If the sub is not sunk or forced to RTB, it may then attack per [14.15]. Then **skip** the counterattack [14.2], already conducted. |
| **8** | **Counterattack** — escorts detect the sub on approach and hold it down. Resolve as Event #7 except **the submarine cannot make an attack**. In a wolfpack, any submarines with **no escorts** in the same or an adjacent column may go on to attack the surface ships. |
| **9** | **Combat - Combat** — the sub eludes all escorts and attacks. **There is no Counterattack.** After the first round the Japanese force is still revealed per [14.4]. |

## [20.0] SPECIAL MISSION ZONES (SMZs)

> **Commentary.** Submarines were often co-opted by higher command for special missions — covering invasions,
> inserting supplies and guerillas, supporting major surface battles, searching for downed airmen, or barring
> enemy movement through a geographic choke point. SMZs see intensive enemy surface activity, increasing both
> the chance to contact targets and the danger of detection and ASW attack.

SMZs are the circular areas on the map **without** Area Activity Charts.

**[20.1]** SMZs may **not** be entered unless **activated on the War Event Table**. Individual SMZs are
activated by a die roll during the Strategic Segment, **for a single turn only**. SMZs are linked by a light
blue arrowhead to one or more OpAreas and may only be entered via a linked OpArea.

**[20.2]** Submarines assigned to an active SMZ are **exempt from Congregating Risk** [22.0] in that OpArea.

**[20.3]** Immediately after arriving in an OpArea, a submarine may be repositioned to an active SMZ connected
by a light blue arrowhead. Doing so requires a **Transit Event check with a +2 drm**, using the AAC of the
OpArea from which the sub entered the SMZ.

**[20.4]** In an SMZ, the submarine conducts the mission given by the War Event. For a Search and Contact
mission, use the AAC of the OpArea it entered from, with **all results increased by one Activity Level**:
Sparse→Low, Low→Moderate, Moderate→High. **High remains High and No Contact remains No Contact.** Other results
may apply as directed by the War Events Table.

**[20.5]** Submarines in an SMZ **skip the Endurance check**. Unless noted below, they are marked **RTB** and
placed in the DONE section of the OpArea they entered from.

**[20.51]** Submarines conducting these War Events must enter RTB status **and** conduct transit movement in the
current turn: **Fall of Luzon**, **Run to Corregidor**, **Fall of the Dutch East Indies**.

Five **major Bases**: **PEARL HARBOR, FREMANTLE, BRISBANE, MANILA/SUBIC BAY, DUTCH HARBOR**, each with a Base

| Dutch Harbor | Active on War Event. Inactive on War Event. |

### [21.1] Forward Bases
Represented on the map by **Submarine Tenders in SMZs**:

| Forward Base | Activation |

### [21.2] Submarine Tenders
Tenders support submarines in forward areas with fuel, torpedoes, food, and supplies. They lack full base
capability but can conduct minor repairs and extend time on station.

**[21.21]** When a Forward Base is activated by the War Event Table, randomly select a submarine tender and
place it in the SMZ during the **War Progress Phase** of that turn unless otherwise noted. A tender may not be
added to the draw pile until its **deployment date**, printed on the counter and the tender display.

**[21.22]** A tender's **support capacity** (on the Submarine Tender card and the counter) is the maximum number
of submarines that can be in its Broom, Shipside, and Ready for Sea Boxes at any one time. At maximum capacity,
no additional submarines may RTB to that tender.

**[21.23]** A **damaged** submarine may RTB to a Forward Base for minor repairs. Roll immediately on the
Submarine Damage Table: on **"Superficial Damage"**, move it to the tender's Broom Box; on any other result,
move it to the **"Done"** section of the applicable OpArea, and in subsequent turns it may only RTB to a
**major** base.

**[21.24]** A submarine tender at a base which falls is removed from play but remains eligible for a new base.

## [22.0] CONGREGATING RISK

> **Commentary.** Congregating many submarines in one operating area was a great risk: radio traffic alerted the
> Japanese to increased activity, and one submarine's actions could draw attention that revealed another. A 1942
> effort of exactly this kind caused a rapid rise in losses, prompting shore commanders to stop "playing
> checkers" with the boats.

There is **no limit** on submarines in an OpArea, but liabilities accrue.

**[22.1]** For **each multiple of four submarines beyond the first four** in an OpArea with **RED or BLUE**
boxes in its AAC for the current War Period, apply to **all** submarines there:
- **A. +1** to any Transit Event die roll if passing through, arriving, or departing.
- **B. −1** when searching for ships on the Area Activity Chart.

**[22.2]** Cumulative for additional multiples of four (nine submarines → −2 search, +2 transit).

**[22.3] Wolfpacks.** Every two submarines (fractions rounded up) in a wolfpack count as **one** submarine for
Congregating Risk.

## [23.0] SPOTTED EFFECTS

A spotted submarine has greater difficulty finding the enemy (who avoids the reported area) and faces increased
risk from Japanese escort forces.

- **War Periods 1 and 2 only:** **−1** on any Area Activity Chart roll, **+1** on any Transit Event roll.
- **Always:** **+1** when rolling on the Counterattack Table per [14.2].

**[23.1]** Additional Spotted markers have no effect — an already-Spotted submarine receiving another Spotted
result takes no action.

## [24.0] WOLFPACKS

> **Commentary.** Despite German success with wolfpacks, the U.S. was at first loath to emulate the tactic. By
> spring 1943 Jimmy Fife, commander of submarines in Brisbane, was willing to try. It took time to develop the
> skill, but by mid-1944 American wolfpacks were accomplishing what the Germans could only dream of.

A wolfpack may only be formed at a **major** sub Base (not a Forward Base). All participants must **start out
from their Base at the same time** and **arrive in the wolfpack's OpArea at the same time**. All wolfpack
submarines are placed under a **Wolfpack marker**. Submarines may leave a wolfpack at any time, but once a
submarine has left, it may not rejoin at sea. **Minimum wolfpack size is three (3).**

### [24.1] Wolfpack Formation
Wolfpacks may be formed starting **October 1943**. The chosen submarines must all be in the **Ready for Sea Box**.

**[24.11]** Before the Wolfpack Experience marker reaches **9** (see [24.7]):
- A. Only **one** wolfpack may be deployed at a time.
- B. Wolfpacks may not exceed **three** submarines.

**[24.12]** The turn after Wolfpack Experience reaches **9**:
- A. Up to **three** wolfpacks at a time.
- B. Wolfpacks may not exceed **four** submarines.

**[24.13]** The turn after Wolfpack Experience reaches **15**:
- A. Up to **five** wolfpacks at a time.
- B. Wolfpacks may not exceed **six** submarines.

**[24.14] Disbanding.** A wolfpack falling below three submarines ceases to exist at the **end of the Combat
Phase** in which it fell below three; the remaining two (or one) may continue to attack together **for the
current phase only**. If the pack falls below three due to a **Transit Event** or a member **failing its
Endurance check**, the pack disbands **immediately**.

> **Commentary.** A wolfpack is more than the sum of its parts. A pair of submarines operating together benefits
> statistically, but is not sufficient to qualify as a wolfpack for rules purposes.

### [24.2] Wolfpacks and Movement
All members move together as if a single unit. Wolfpacks **cannot transfer from Base to Base**, but they may
"bounce" to a new base after spending at least one turn in **patrol** mode.

### [24.3] Wolfpack Types

> **Commentary.** American wolfpacks operated in close proximity — less area covered, but a quick coordinated
> attack. German wolfpacks operated as a barrier, often stretched hundreds of miles, giving higher detection
> probability but often leaving the farther boats unable to catch the convoy.

Wolfpacks deploy in an OpArea or SMZ as **Group** or **Barrier**. The player decides which **before** the Search
and Contact Phase [13.0].

**[24.31] Group Pack** — the pack makes a **single** AAC die roll [13.2.A] with **no modifier**.

**[24.32] Barrier Pack** — the pack makes a **single** AAC die roll with a **+2** modifier. If contact is made,
one randomly chosen submarine is assumed to have made it. A separate die roll is then made for each additional
submarine: **0–5** it intercepts; **6–9** it cannot and is set aside (still in the wolfpack). At the conclusion
of the turn, all surviving wolfpack submarines — including non-intercepting ones — that do not have to RTB are
placed in the **"Done"** section of the OpArea.

### [24.4] Wolfpacks and Transit Events
Each member rolls **separately** for Transit Events as normal, but **each subtracts one (−1)**. Should a member
encounter a Transit Event, the remaining rolls to resolve that event are **normal, unmodified** rolls.

**[24.41]** A member sustaining an **RTB/DAMAGE** result must drop out and return to Base — this may force the
pack to disband. A member that is sunk is removed from play.

### [24.5] Wolfpacks and Combat
All wolfpack members go on the Combat Display. The player may freely assign submarines to columns, **no more
than two subs per column**.

**[24.51]** If a **Diligent Escort** counter is drawn, its attack is made on a **randomly chosen** submarine
before placing it in its proper column.

**[24.52] Combat Events** [19.2] apply **only to the submarine that reveals the event chit**.

**[24.53] Sequence** — each submarine resolves attack and counterattack individually, one at a time:
1. Assign subs to columns.
2. Starting with the sub of your choice, reveal Japanese units per [14.12].
3. With the same sub, place TDC markers per [14.13].
4. Make the attack with that sub, using the current **Revealed Escort level**.
5. Resolve the counterattack on that sub.
6. Return to step 2 with sub #2, and so on.

**[24.54] Revealed Escort Level** — each submarine has its own level, applying to both attack and counterattack:
- a. An escort **in the sub's column** → its ASW factor applies to that submarine.
- b. An escort in an **adjacent column with no submarine in it** → its ASW factor applies to that submarine.
- c. An **aircraft's** ASW level applies to **all** submarines regardless of column.

> *Note:* This can result in one escort's ASW factor being applied to up to **four** submarines.

**[24.55] Re-Attack.** Wolfpacks may Re-Attack **once but never twice** (coordination breaks down). Each member
must roll for it: roll a die; if **= or <** the submarine's **tactical rating plus any Super Skipper value**, it
may participate. **Damaged submarines add +2** to this roll. Failure means that submarine **withdraws** [14.3]
from the current combat phase but remains a member of the wolfpack.

### [24.6] Wolfpacks and SPOTTED Results
Any SPOTTED result obtained by a member spots **all** members. In the Conclude Turn Phase, **one** die roll is
made for the entire pack: on removal, markers come off all submarines in the pack; otherwise the entire pack
remains spotted.

### [24.7] Wolfpack Experience
When **two or more subs in a wolfpack sink an enemy ship in any one Combat Phase** [14.0], the wolfpack gains a
**Wolfpack Experience Point** (the marker advances one on the tonnage track). **A wolfpack may only gain one
experience point per patrol.**
- At **9**, the player's ability to deploy and assign submarines to wolfpacks improves.
- At **15**, that ability reaches its maximum.

## [25.0] SUPER SKIPPERS

> **Commentary.** Legendary skippers such as Morton, O'Kane, and Klakring established a tradition of excellence
> for those who would wear silver or gold dolphins. SILENT WAR provides **twenty-four** named skippers, two of
> them Allied.

### [25.1] Super Skipper Determination *(also [14.5])*
If a submarine sank **three (3) or more ships totaling 16,000 or more tons** during the Combat Phase, it has a
Super Skipper:
- No Super Skipper → randomly draw and assign one at **+1**.
- Already **+1** → flip to **+2**.
- Already **+2** → no additional benefit.

**[25.2]** The Super Skipper normally remains with one submarine and is **lost if the submarine is lost**.

**[25.3] Reassignment** is only possible when the submarine is **withdrawn from service** (removed by War Event
result, or declared a constructive total loss per [18.1]/[18.4]). Roll a die for the Skipper:
- **0–4** → removed from play (training command, a surface warship, or the War Department).
- **5–9** → may be assigned at the **+1** level to any submarine currently in a Repair Box, or any submarine next
  in line to be received as a reinforcement or returning from overhaul.

> *Example:* In December 1943, S-42 with +2 Whitaker is withdrawn from active service. The player rolls an 8, so
> +1 Whitaker may be reassigned to any sub in a Repair Box, or to *Redfin, Angler, Flasher, Robalo, Tang, Jack,*
> or *Tambor*, which arrive in January 1944.

## [26.0] TWO PLAYER VARIANT

> **Commentary.** Although there was one ComSubPac, there were in reality two functional commands: Pacific
> Command at Pearl Harbor and SouthWest Pacific Command at Fremantle and Brisbane.

Two players may play simultaneously, each taking one command. **This is cooperative, not competitive** — the
distribution of submarines, AAC variance, and the distance between commands make artificial balance
impractical. All game rules are followed normally; both players play each turn with the submarines at their
disposal. Transfers are by player agreement when not specified in the rules.

*Suggested cooperation:* whenever the number of submarines operating from Australia falls below thirty-six, the
SouthWest Pacific Commander may demand six of the newest class of submarines available to the Pearl Harbor
Command. Players should otherwise balance distribution and agree on operating areas. Both may hunt in the same
OpArea, but **Congregating Risk [22.0] applies**.

## [27.0] QUICK RESOLUTION

An expedited combat resolution. Players should be fully familiar with normal combat first. Valid for occasional
use, but **not recommended for extensive campaign use** — results are approximate and can vary greatly from
historical results over a war.

**Procedure.** Proceed normally through [13.1]. Instead of continuing to [13.2]:

1. Roll the die **twice**. If the result is **0,0**, conduct Sub vs. Sub combat [14.6] and stop this sequence.
2. Calculate the submarine's attack value: **attack factor + any Super Skipper value + current Mk-XIV torpedo
   value**, then **−1 if damaged** and **−1 if spotted** (maximum subtraction −2).
3. Determine the **number of colored (non-white) boxes** in the applicable war period line of the OpArea chart,
   and cross-index the two numbers on the **Quick Resolution Table**.
4. Roll one die, subtracting **−1** if this is an ULTRA area, **−1** if the submarine has radar (1943–45), and
   **−1** if the submarine is a member of a wolfpack (maximum subtraction −3).
5. **Subtract this number from the Quick Resolution Table value.** The result is the **number of ships sunk**.
   *(Table value 3, die roll 2 → one ship sunk.)*
6. Draw the first ship from **Cup A**, the second from **Cup B**, third **Cup C**, fourth **Cup D**, fifth
   **Cup A**, etc. Remember the last cup drawn from; the next combat sequence begins with the following cup.
7. **All ships drawn are sunk** except Diligent Escorts.
8. If a **Combat Event** marker is drawn, resolve it immediately before further draws. **Disregard all events
   except** "Circular running torpedo", "Betty Bomber spots submarine", and "Mechanical Troubles End Attack".
9. If a **Diligent Escort** is drawn, roll immediately on the "Diligent Escort" line of the Counterattack Table.
10. If an **aircraft** is drawn, roll immediately on the "Event Aircraft" line of the Counterattack Table.

### [27.1] Quick Resolution Counter Attack
Roll a die, **subtract one-half (rounded up) of the submarine's defense value**, and find the colored box in the
appropriate war period line of the OpArea Activity Chart:

| Box color | Result |
|---|---|
| White | Combat for the round ends |
| Green | The submarine is **spotted** |
| Orange | The submarine must **RTB** |
| Blue | The submarine is **damaged** |
| Red | The submarine is **sunk** |

**+1** to the die roll if the submarine began the combat damaged.

## [28.0] RULES FROM THE IJN EXPANSION KIT

*Steel Wolves, Vol 1* introduced several new concepts to the Silent War system. Those rules were incorporated
into **SILENT WAR: Imperial Japanese Navy, Expansion Pack 1**, and are added here for completeness. The IJN
numbering convention is retained for cross-referencing.

### [IJN 4.0] LONERS

Unlike the Atlantic, the vast majority of Japanese shipping was organized into task-force-style convoys.
However, loners did occur, especially lone warships.

#### [4.1] Determining Loner Contacts
When rolling for contacts on an AAC War Period row **that has red boxes** and the result is a **white box**,
there is a chance of a loner. On a white box result, **roll again**. If the second roll's result is a **red box**
on that AAC's War Period line, a **loner has been encountered**.

**[4.11]** If the initial roll is a **zero**, the second roll is **also** used to determine whether there is a
Sub vs. Sub contact [SW14.6].

#### [4.2] Setting Up Loner Combat

**The Target.** Draw a single ship from a cup according to the current War Period:

| War Period | Cup |
|---|---|
| WP1 | A |
| WP2 | B |
| WP3 | C |
| WP4 | D |

Place the ship in the column associated with the cup it was drawn from (to remember its origin).

**The Submarine.** Roll one die, subtracting any Super Skipper value, and compare to the submarine's **tactical
rating**. If the modified roll is **= or <** the tactical rating, place the submarine in the **same column** as
the target; otherwise place it in an **adjacent column**.

#### [4.3] Resolving Loner Combat
Reveal the target unit.

- **Combat Event** → roll per [SW19.2], but **only apply the event on a result of 3 or 4**; treat 0, 1, 2, 5, 6,
  7, 8, 9 as "no-event".
- **Aircraft** (not an Aircraft Capable Ship per [IJN 3.2]) → the aircraft receives a **single counterattack
  round**, after which combat ends.
- **Merchant** (green bar on yellow) → proceed to the Combat Procedure.
- **Japanese Warship** (grey bar on white) or **non-warship Auxiliary** (grey bar on yellow) → follow the normal
  IJN Set rules [3.1] and proceed to the Combat Procedure.

**Combat Procedure.** Resolve combat normally, except the player may **voluntarily invoke Combat Event #1
(Surface Gunnery Combat)** unless the loner is a naval unit (Japanese Warship; non-warship Auxiliaries **may**
be attacked by surface gunnery). Re-attack rounds may be used and a Super Skipper may get an additional
re-attack per [SW14.41]. The player subtracts **−1** on the combat Endurance Check at the end of combat.

### [IJN 5.0] CRASH DIVE!

The **Crash Dive Rating** gives a submarine a "second chance" against sudden attacks — patrol aircraft, diligent
escorts, and malfunctioning torpedoes.

#### [5.1] Crash Dive Procedure
During combat resolution, if a **Diligent Escort** is drawn, or the Combat Events **"Circular Running Torpedo"**
or **"Betty Bomber Spots Submarine"** are rolled, the submarine may attempt to evade. **Before** resolving the
escort, aircraft, or circular torpedo, roll one die, subtract any Super Skipper rating, and compare to the
**Crash Dive Rating**:

- **Roll = or < Crash Dive Rating → success.** Roll on the Diligent Escort or Event Aircraft Table as
  applicable, but change any result except "No Effect" to **"Spotted"**. Circular running torpedoes
  **automatically miss** a successfully crash-dived submarine — continue combat as normal. If diving to avoid a
  Betty bomber, proceed to the Endurance Check as directed by that Combat Event.
- **Failure** → the escort or event is resolved normally and combat continues normally.

|---|---|---|---|

| S2 | 0 | Gato | 0 |
| S3 | 0 | Balao | 0 |

| B | 0 | Scheme 17 | 0 |
| Argonaut | X | K-VIII | 0 |
| Narwhal | X | K-XI | 0 |
| Dolphin | 0 | K-XIV | 0 |

| P1 | 0 | O-19 | 0 |

### [IJN 6.0] TARGETING AN ESCORT

Most successful attacks on escorts occurred when the escort was unaware until the torpedo hit. This option
allows a 10–20% improved chance of hitting **one** escort, in line with historical occurrence. The escort's high
defense value must still be overcome and a good TDC solution is still needed.

**Applies only to the first attack round** (not the re-attack). A submarine may target **one (1)** escort, and
that escort's **ASW value is not counted as part of its defense**. The ASW values of any other revealed escorts
**are** counted. The option may be used when firing at an unrevealed target that later turns out to be an
escort, so long as **only one escort in total** has its ASW value ignored. If the escort is damaged or sunk, its
ASW value is not used in the counterattack round.

#### [6.1] Restrictions — cannot be used if:
- a. The submarine is **SPOTTED**.
- b. A **Diligent Escort** has been revealed.
- c. An **aircraft** has been revealed.
- d. Any of these Combat Events occur: **Betty Bomber Spots Submarine**; **Counterattack** (escorts spot sub and
  hold it down); **Counterattack - Combat** (escorts attack before sub can).
- e. A member of a **wolfpack** has already attacked, alerting the escorts — only the **first** member of a pack
  can take advantage.

### [IJN 7.0] EARLY ADVANCEMENT TO WAR PERIOD 4

> **Commentary.** By 1945 Japanese imports had collapsed: bauxite −88%, iron ore −95%, wood pulp −90%, cotton
> and wool −91%, oil −92%, soda and cement −96%, lumber −98%, rubber −99%. Consequences included aircraft engine
> testing falling from 8 hours/five flights per engine to 2 hours of ground testing on only 10% of engines; 80%
> of each aircraft made from substandard aluminum pilings by end-1944; sharply reduced pilot training from fuel
> shortage; carriers reluctant to leave port for training in submarine-infested waters; naval units stationed
> near fuel rather than where effective; 1944 steel shortages cutting naval and merchant construction and
> repairs; and island garrison delivery rates falling from 96% (1942) to 67% (1944) to 51% (1945).

An adjustment to [SW17.1] allowing early transition to War Period 4 when the player outperforms history. During
the War Progress Phase, **instead of** the printed War Period Transition Table numbers for War Period 4 (June 44
– Jan 45):

**Procedure.** When the **Ships Sunk marker reaches or exceeds 860** *and* the **Tons Sunk marker reaches or
exceeds 3,800 (3.8 million) tons**, the **current month becomes the new first month** for the transition to War
Period 4. The current month is treated as containing **'9'** (as printed for June 1944), and subsequent months
take the corresponding subsequent numbers. Thereafter, rolling on the War Period Modification Table for War
Period 4 proceeds normally.

> *Example:* In March 1944 the player achieves 860 ships sunk for 3,900t. War Period 4 transition advances into
> the current month: March = '9', April = '8', May = '7', and so on.
